# H-007: Cross-Instrument Correlation Structure

## Prior evidence (recorded before test)
None from this project directly. Per `research/S1_INTERIM_FINDING_5_KILLS.md`'s
committed budget, this is the third and final of the pre-committed
mechanism families to be tested before the re-evaluation point. Chosen
over "momentum/mean-reversion at alternate horizons" because it does
double duty: it also directly informs the portfolio-null extension the
two-year vision review flagged as underweighted ("weight the portfolio
problem higher... correlation-aware combination testing should enter
the roadmap the moment there are two acceptances, not in some later
portfolio phase"). There are currently zero acceptances, but the
measurement itself -- do these five instruments actually move
independently enough for future diversification to mean anything -- is
useful groundwork regardless of whether any hypothesis ever survives.

## IMPORTANT: this hypothesis has a different evidentiary form than H-001 through H-006
Every prior hypothesis in this registry tested whether the SIGNED NULL
MODEL's random-entry P&L differs across some grouping (session,
volatility, day-of-week), using permutation tests and a seed-to-seed
dispersion check to guard against pseudoreplication. This hypothesis
is NOT a null-model test -- it is a direct, deterministic measurement
of the real price data's own return correlation structure. There is no
seed, no permutation, no random draw to be pseudoreplicated. The
FDR-adjustment and dispersion-check machinery from H-002/H-004/H-005/
H-006 does not mechanically apply here, and this registration does not
pretend it does. What DOES carry over is the core discipline: the
threshold for "correlated enough to matter" is committed BEFORE the
correlation matrix is computed, not chosen after looking at the
numbers.

## Prediction (the falsifiable form)
The five instruments' H1 bar-to-bar returns, over the TRAIN window
(2019-01-01 to 2022-01-01), have an average pairwise absolute
correlation below 0.5. This is the threshold below which the two-year
vision review's premise -- that a portfolio of several small,
decorrelated effects is more achievable than one strong effect --
remains plausible on this specific instrument set. Above 0.5 average
pairwise correlation, that premise is weaker than assumed, and should
be revisited explicitly rather than carried forward silently.

Also reported, non-adjudicated (descriptive): the full 5x5 pairwise
correlation matrix, and specifically whether the two JPY-cross pairs
(USDJPY, GBPJPY) and the two USD-cross pairs (EURUSD, AUDUSD) show
higher correlation WITHIN their currency-sharing pairs than across --
an economically expected pattern if the measurement is behaving
sensibly, not a claim requiring its own kill criteria.

## Test design
- Data: TRAIN window only (2019-01-01 -> 2022-01-01), H1 bars, all 5
  instruments (`data/storage/{SYMBOL}_16385.csv`, already ingested and
  hash-pinned in S1).
- Returns: simple bar-to-bar log returns on `close`, computed
  independently per instrument, then inner-joined on timestamp
  (instruments may have slightly different bar availability; only
  timestamps common to all 5 are used, to avoid spurious correlation
  from misaligned padding/forward-fill).
- Metric: Pearson correlation, pairwise, all 10 unique pairs among the
  5 instruments. Primary metric: mean of the 10 pairwise |correlation|
  values.
- No permutation test, no seed dispersion check (see note above -- not
  applicable to a deterministic price-data measurement). No new
  simulation, no null model -- this is the cheapest possible hypothesis
  in this registry to test.

## Kill criteria (what would refute the prediction)
- Mean pairwise |correlation| >= 0.5 across the 10 pairs -> the
  portfolio-diversification premise is weaker than assumed for this
  instrument set; report as a finding, not a failure of the search
  process itself.
- If the JPY-cross pair and USD-cross pair do NOT show the expected
  higher within-group correlation pattern, flag this explicitly as a
  possible data or methodology issue rather than silently accepting an
  uninterpretable result.

## What I will NOT do
- Apply the FDR-adjusted p-value threshold or dispersion check
  mechanically to a measurement that has no p-value or seed dimension
  -- doing so would be a category error, not rigor.
- Choose the 0.5 threshold after seeing the correlation matrix.
- Treat a "correlated" result as a failure of this hypothesis in the
  same sense H-001 through H-006's kills were failures -- high
  correlation is a real, useful finding about the instrument universe,
  not evidence the testing methodology or search process is broken.
- Unseal OOS (not applicable here in the usual sense, since this
  measures TRAIN-window price structure directly, but the same
  discipline of not looking at 2022-2025 data applies).
